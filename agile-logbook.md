# PowerShare – Agile Logbook

## 1. Introduction

This logbook records my learning and progress while preparing the Agile portfolio for PowerShare. PowerShare is an affordable, pay-as-you-go portable energy rental service intended to help micro-entrepreneurs in Khayelitsha and Mitchells Plain continue essential business activities during power outages.

The project focuses on small businesses such as barbershops, tailoring businesses and spaza shops, which can lose customers and income when electricity is unavailable.

The purpose of this logbook is to reflect on the activities involved in understanding the problem, identifying user needs, prioritising requirements and planning development work.

## 2. Agile Log Entries

### Entry 1: Understanding the Project Problem

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Understanding the PowerShare business problem.

**Progress:**

I identified the main problem PowerShare aims to address: frequent power outages can interrupt business operations and reduce daily income. The project focuses on micro-entrepreneurs in Khayelitsha and Mitchells Plain who may not be able to afford to purchase their own backup power equipment.

I established that PowerShare aims to provide portable lithium power stations through an affordable, pay-as-you-go rental service.

**Challenge:**

One challenge was narrowing the project focus to the needs of the target users instead of trying to solve every problem associated with electricity outages.

**Learning:**

I learned that understanding the problem and the people affected by it is an important first step in developing a useful solution. Project features should address real user needs.

### Entry 2: Preparing the Agile Team Charter

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Preparing the PowerShare Agile Team Charter.

**Progress:**

I prepared a team charter outlining the project purpose, goals, team values, working agreements and Agile approach. The team consists of Refilwe, Mojalefa and Magdeline.

The charter provides a structure for documenting how the team should communicate, share responsibilities and work towards the project goals.

**Challenge:**

A challenge was ensuring that the charter remained focused on PowerShare and its target users. Another consideration was making sure that team responsibilities accurately reflect the roles agreed upon by the members.

**Learning:**

I learned that a team charter helps establish shared expectations. Clear responsibilities and communication can reduce confusion and help team members understand their contributions.

### Entry 3: Developing User Stories and Tasks

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Identifying user stories for PowerShare.

**Progress:**

I developed proposed user stories covering viewing available power stations, checking rental prices, booking a power station, tracking rental requests and managing equipment availability.

I also identified acceptance criteria and smaller tasks for selected stories. These help explain what a feature should achieve and what work may be required to deliver it.

**Challenge:**

The main challenge was translating the broad idea of an energy rental service into specific requirements that could be understood and developed individually.

**Learning:**

I learned that user stories describe requirements from the user's perspective. Acceptance criteria help clarify when a requirement can be considered complete, while tasks break the work into manageable activities.

### Entry 4: Preparing the Product Backlog

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Organising and prioritising PowerShare requirements.

**Progress:**

I prepared a proposed product backlog containing features such as viewing available power stations, understanding rental prices, requesting rentals, checking request status and managing equipment availability.

The features were grouped into high, medium and low priorities to show which requirements are most important to the core service.

**Challenge:**

A challenge was deciding which features should receive the highest priority. The team needs to focus first on requirements that support the main rental process while considering the time and resources available.

**Learning:**

I learned that the product backlog helps organise project requirements and supports decision-making. Priorities should be reviewed as the project develops and more information becomes available.

### Entry 5: Preparing the Sprint Backlog

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Planning proposed sprint work.

**Progress:**

I prepared a proposed sprint backlog focused on the core rental process. The selected work included planning the power station listings, displaying rental prices and preparing a rental request process.

The tasks were broken into smaller activities to make the work easier to understand and organise.

**Challenge:**

One challenge was distinguishing between the complete product backlog and the smaller amount of work that could realistically be selected for a single sprint.

**Learning:**

I learned that a sprint backlog connects the sprint goal to specific tasks. It should reflect the team's actual plan and be updated as work progresses.

### Entry 6: Reviewing Portfolio Evidence

**Date:** 22 August 2026  
**Activity:** Reviewing the evidence required for the Agile portfolio.

**Progress:**

I reviewed the five portfolio sections: the team charter, user stories and tasks, product backlog, sprint backlog and Agile logbook. I also identified the need to include screenshots and reflections as supporting evidence.

**Challenge:**

A challenge was understanding the difference between written documentation and evidence from an actual project management tool. A written backlog describes planned work, while a genuine screenshot can demonstrate how the work is recorded in a tool.

**Learning:**

I learned that evidence should support the claims made in project documentation. Screenshots, task records and relevant commit history should reflect actual project activities.

## 3. Overall Reflection

Preparing the PowerShare Agile portfolio has helped me understand how different Agile artefacts support software project planning.

The team charter establishes shared expectations, user stories explain what users need, the product backlog organises and prioritises requirements, and the sprint backlog identifies the work selected for a particular sprint. The Agile logbook provides an opportunity to reflect on progress, challenges and learning.

I also learned that a proposed feature is not necessarily an implemented feature. Requirements, plans and completed work should be recorded accurately so that the portfolio provides a clear picture of the project's progress.

An important area for improvement is ensuring that project records are updated consistently and that screenshots and other evidence are collected as activities take place.

## 4. Conclusion

The PowerShare Agile Logbook records my understanding of the project's problem, its intended users and the purpose of the main Agile artefacts.

This process has strengthened my understanding of requirements analysis, prioritisation, sprint planning and reflection. These practices can help a development team stay organised and focus on delivering a solution that responds to user needs.
